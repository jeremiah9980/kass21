About #21

Kassidy Cargill is a rising softball athlete whose game is built on toughness, versatility, speed, and impact. What first looked like a natural catcher profile expanded into something even more dynamic: a multi-position player who can make her presence felt in the field, on the bases, and at the plate.

Across her career, Kassidy has developed into a high-contact hitter with real pressure-player value. She owns a .449 career batting average, .520 OBP, 1.224 OPS, 97 career hits, and 74 stolen bases with a 94.87% stolen-base success rate.

Player Timeline

2023 • Foundation

Early development in Georgetown beginning in boys baseball at NAO, then transitioning through a difficult GYGSA softball experience before landing on Romo's Arsenal (GYBA). Established catcher instincts and utility identity across both leagues. Helped Arsenal win the opening tournament, take the #1 regular-season spot, and claim the end-of-season tournament — a triple championship in her first year of competitive baseball.

2024 • Dual-Sport Growth

Played Panthers 10U D1 baseball while also competing with Bananas softball, accelerating athletic development through cross-sport reps. Faster reactions, stronger instincts, and a bigger all-around game emerged from the simultaneous competition environment. Posted a .548 AVG with the Bananas in Spring 2024 and began building her stolen-base profile in earnest (27 SB in the fall slate).

2025 • Breakout Progression

Transitioned into the Hotshots program and emerged as a high-impact offensive player. Spring 2025 stands as her peak offensive season: .426 AVG, .806 SLG, 1.297 OPS, 6 home runs, and 45 stolen bases. Extra-base power and basepath pressure combined into a complete offensive profile. Her identity as a utility athlete with elite contact and speed solidified in this season.

Spring 2026 • Current Season

18–6 team record with continued offensive production. Posting a .389 AVG, .522 OBP, 1.188 OPS with a perfect 1.000 BB/K ratio and .421 BA with runners in scoring position. Five hard-hit balls and strong contact rate (80.56%) demonstrate a maturing approach at the plate.
